### The business of sport, for allocators — week of Aug 17 – Aug 19, 2026

## Lead: Capital is consolidating around durable, diversified sports assets — and abandoning single-bet, cash-intensive ones

Two pairs of stories from this short cycle tell the same story from opposite ends. In English football, Chelsea's Todd Boehly and Mark Walter are reportedly in talks to sell their combined 25.6% stake (12.8% each) to Clearlake Capital, the club's existing 61.5% majority owner — a clean, negotiated consolidation inside an established Premier League asset, with no price yet disclosed. The same week, Sheffield United's American ownership group is fighting to keep the club at all: former owner United World alleges $47M of the original $135M purchase price — roughly a third of the deal — remains unpaid, and that the buyers moved shares into a new holding company to dodge the balance. If a court agrees, the current owners lose the club, and it takes a 12-point league penalty with it. One is a majority owner buying more of an asset it already controls; the other is a control dispute triggered by an unpaid obligation on a smaller, more fragile one.

The same pattern shows up a day later in the numbers. Amer Sports — the diversified owner of Arc'teryx, Salomon, and Wilson — posted 32% revenue growth to $1.63B and raised its full-year outlook. Enhanced Games, the single-event sports venture Peter Thiel backed to a $1.2B IPO valuation in May, posted a $62M quarterly loss driven largely by the cost of its own debut Las Vegas event, and its shares are down 84% for the year. Diversified, cash-generative sports and culture businesses are getting rewarded this cycle; concentrated, capital-intensive ones are getting punished. For anyone underwriting sports-adjacent equity, that's the read to carry into the rest of this edition.

*(via The Athletic, Front Office Sports)*

## Europe

**Chelsea's co-owners are negotiating an exit to their own majority shareholder** — Boehly and Walter are reportedly in talks to sell their 12.8% stakes each (25.6% combined) to Clearlake Capital, which already holds 61.5% of the club the trio bought for £2.3B in 2022. No price has been disclosed. The move follows reported strain between the partners, and comes shortly after Walter sold his stake in the Los Angeles Lakers for $12.5B while facing a federal probe into his insurance holdings. **Stryde angle:** this is an in-syndicate consolidation, not a new capital event — no fresh valuation is being set, since the buyer already controls the club. Worth watching for whether a price ever surfaces publicly, since it would be one of the few recent data points on where Premier League minority stakes actually clear. *(via The Athletic)*

**Sheffield United's owners could lose the club over an unpaid $47M balance** — United World, the club's former ownership group, has petitioned a court alleging the current American buyers still owe $47M of the original $135M purchase price and that they transferred shares into a new holding company to avoid paying it. If the court grants the petition, the buyers lose control of the club and it faces a 12-point league deduction. **Stryde angle:** the unpaid balance functions like a vendor loan in default — a large deferred-consideration structure that didn't get funded, not a straightforward equity dispute. The share-transfer allegation is unproven; if it holds up, it's a governance red flag for any deal structured with material deferred payments to a prior owner. *(via The Athletic)*

**Amer Sports raised its outlook after a strong quarter** — the owner of Arc'teryx, Salomon, and Wilson posted 32% revenue growth to $1.63B and $127M in profit, driven by its outdoor and athletic brands, and now expects 24% full-year revenue growth. **Stryde angle:** the clean counter-comp to Enhanced Games below — a diversified, multi-brand sports portfolio compounding profitably while a single-event venture burns cash on its own launch. Worth using as the reference point when a concentrated sports bet gets pitched as a growth story. *(via a wire report)*

## Americas

**NBA ad revenue jumped 38% in the first year of its new media deal** — advertisers spent $2.11B on NBA games in 2025-26, up from $1.53B, after the league shifted games to broadcast TV on NBC and ABC (lifting average viewership 16% to 1.78M) and added Amazon Prime Video as a new partner, which alone sold $507.4M in ads to a meaningfully younger audience. **Stryde angle:** a step-change tied to a new rights cycle starting, not organic growth — the real test is whether this ad-revenue level holds once the new-deal novelty wears off. *(via Sportico)*

**A new wave of unregulated NIL agents is charging up to 20% commission — nearly seven times what NFL agents are capped at** — Texas-based A&P Sports Agency has negotiated over $30M in college athlete deals as direct school payments replace endorsement deals, while union-regulated NFL agents are capped at 3%. Lawmakers have proposed the Protect College Sports Act to cap NIL agency fees at 5%. **Stryde angle:** a real regulatory-arbitrage story — college sports' NIL market operates outside the fee-cap regime governing every major pro league, and that gap is exactly what the proposed legislation targets. Worth tracking as a near-term regulatory-risk marker for anything with NIL exposure. *(via The Athletic)*

**Enhanced Games posted a $62M quarterly loss after its debut event** — the Peter Thiel-backed sports and health venture, which went public in May at a $1.2B valuation, lost the money largely on the cost of hosting its first Las Vegas competition; its shares have fallen 84% this year, and it says it will scale back to smaller events while leaning more on its telehealth and supplements divisions. **Stryde angle:** see the lead — this is the cash-intensive, single-event side of this cycle's contrast with Amer Sports, and the pivot toward telehealth and supplements reads as the company trying to buy itself a more diversified, less capital-hungry revenue base after the fact. *(via Front Office Sports)*

**Women's sports advertising spend is up 120% since 2022** — Nielsen reports 28.6B minutes of women's sports viewership in early 2026 (+18% year over year), pushing WNBA sponsor values up 71% and drawing 16M new fans who are unusually willing to switch brands on corporate values. **Stryde angle:** a structural demand signal, not a one-off — sustained multi-year ad-spend growth alongside audience growth is the combination that actually supports higher media-rights and sponsorship valuations going forward. *(via a wire report)*

**Dick's Sporting Goods is moving into the individual trading-card market** — the retailer is buying cards at trade shows to build hobby shops inside its stores, chasing a market that earned rival Target $1B last year, and recently paid $1.11M for a rare rookie card at auction to generate attention. **Stryde angle:** a low-capital way for a public retailer to add a high-margin collectibles line onto existing store footprint — worth watching as a comp for how legacy retail is trying to capture collectibles-market growth without buying a standalone card company. *(via The Athletic)*

## Global & Cross-Border

**Formula One's owner is pushing further into the US to reach younger audiences** — Liberty Media is expanding American race dates, experimenting with new formats, and has signed unconventional distribution deals, including broadcasts on Apple and a reported $600M Hollywood film, arguing the sport's aging European base can't carry it long-term on its own. **Stryde angle:** the film budget aside, this is a media-rights and audience-development bet, not a new financing event — the real test is whether the American push shows up in F1's own rights valuations at the next renewal, not in this year's ticket sales. *(via SportsPro)*

**The NFL confirmed it expects to eventually operate teams outside the United States** — Commissioner Roger Goodell said he has "zero doubt" international franchises are coming, with the league scheduling a record nine overseas games in 2026 across cities including Paris and Melbourne. Goodell said a city must successfully host a permanent franchise before the league would consider an overseas Super Bowl. **Stryde angle:** a long-dated optionality story, not a near-term one — the sequencing Goodell described (permanent franchise before Super Bowl abroad) is itself the signal for how far out any international-franchise valuation upside actually sits. *(via The Athletic)*
