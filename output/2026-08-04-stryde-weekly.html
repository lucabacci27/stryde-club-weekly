Stryde Club Weekly

The business of sport, for allocators

Week of July 29 – August 4, 2026

Lead

FIFA's $4.2B stake sale collapses — and takes Infantino's grip with it

FIFA spent the week trying, and failing, to sell a 21% stake in its commercial operations to a Joshua Kushner–led investor group for $4.2B — money it wanted to triple development funding for member federations. UEFA threatened a full boycott, Concacaf followed, and by Sunday the deal was dead.

The bigger story for allocators is what it exposed: national federations, including England's FA, have now pulled their backing for Gianni Infantino's 2027 re-election bid. A governing body this size trying to monetize itself via a private stake sale — and getting overruled by its own members — is a live case study in how far "federation as an investable asset" still is from reality, and how much political capital a deal like this can burn even when the check is real.

via The Athletic (nytimes.com)

Capital & Deals

IG Group buys Underdog for up to $1.3B

The London-listed trading firm is paying at least $1.1B cash plus up to $850M in earn-outs for the prediction-market app, which just turned profitable on 950K+ monthly users. A traditional financial-markets player buying its way into sports prediction, rather than building — worth watching as the read-through for how betting-adjacent platforms get valued when a regulated acquirer sets the price.

via Sportico

LIV Golf is down to its last $350M ask

With the Saudi PIF confirmed to be ending its backing after this season, LIV cancelled its $40M Michigan finale and is now shopping for outside capital to survive into 2027. A sovereign-backed league losing its sponsor is the scenario every allocator underwriting a similarly-funded property should be modeling.

via Front Office Sports

Prediction markets keep raising, and keep getting sued

Sports exchange ProphetX closed a $35M round from Parlay Capital and Data Point Capital after 50% user growth in 30 days — the same week New York filed a $36B suit against rival Kalshi alleging illegal gambling. The capital is real; so is the regulatory overhang.

via Google News, Front Office Sports

DAZN's own backer is looking for the exit

Aser Ventures — which holds roughly 5% of DAZN, worth about $400M, from the 2023 Eleven Group deal — is exploring a sale of its stake to fund bets elsewhere. DAZN itself lost $936M last year and is weighing a public listing. A minority holder heading for the door ahead of a potential IPO is a signal worth tracking on valuation.

via Google News

Spanish women's soccer took the cash, Real Madrid wouldn't

Liga F accepted $64.5M from a Pau Gasol–led investor group in exchange for nearly half of broadcast and sponsorship revenue for 25 years. Real Madrid publicly opposed the structure as a bad long-term trade — a useful data point on how top clubs price revenue-share deals against growth capital.

via The Athletic

NBA Europe's London franchise is drawing serious money

Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora is leading a $1B+ bid for the league's proposed London team, one of 120+ bids fielded (range: $500M–$1B+) for the 16-team European league launching in 2027. Franchise pricing this aggressive for a league that doesn't exist yet says a lot about where global sports capital thinks growth is.

Valuations & Ownership

Real Madrid crossed €1.2B in revenue — and still runs on credit

The club is the first to break that mark, driven by its renovated stadium, but holds only €83M in cash and leans on credit lines to cover payroll despite a €26M profit. Scale doesn't equal balance-sheet strength — worth remembering when comping club economics off top-line revenue alone.

via The Athletic

Aston Martin's brand IP is now a credit fight

Creditors owed roughly $1.75B are threatening to unwind a $738M BlackRock/HPS loan that would hand 50.1% of the carmaker's non-car branding rights to Authentic Brands. Structuring a rescue loan against brand IP rather than the core business is an aggressive template — expect to see it copied, and expect the fights that come with it.

via Google News

Sportradar's data costs are outgrowing its revenue

Despite 18.9% revenue growth, the cost of buying sports data rights jumped 29.7% to $159M, swinging the company to a $4M quarterly loss. As rights costs keep compounding, the platforms reselling that data absorb the squeeze first.

via CompleteIGaming

Two venues, two very different funding models

The Rose Bowl raised $80M privately (donations plus local tax credits, no city money) ahead of LA28, while Dublin's Laya Arena completed a €52M joint redevelopment between venue owner RDS and Leinster Rugby with naming rights attached. Two clean templates for venue capex that skips public financing.

via Forbes, SportForBusiness

Leagues & Governance

Private equity now runs the league, not just the teams

Apollo executive and LAFC co-owner Larry Berg was chosen as MLS's next commissioner, beating a former Fox media executive — in a league where 16 of 30 teams already carry PE backing. Berg has to divest his LAFC stake to take the job, but the signal is clear: financial-sponsor DNA now runs league leadership, not just cap tables.

Congress is close to setting the rules on athlete pay

The Big Ten and SEC are backing a Senate bill that would raise the athlete payment cap above $40M and shield the NCAA's governing structure from lawsuits — while a federal judge simultaneously blocked the NCAA from denying a fifth year of eligibility to college athletes. Whichever way this lands changes the cost structure of every college athletics balance sheet.

Women's sports capital is getting crowded, fast

Athletes Unlimited paused its planned 2027 basketball league after the WNBA's new labor deal pushed minimum salaries to $270K and rivals Unrivaled and Project B started offering multi-million-dollar contracts. Capital is chasing women's basketball from several directions at once — the category has moved from thesis to competitive land-grab.

Regional note: the NBA pulled its Abu Dhabi preseason games

The league cancelled its games there over the ongoing US-Iran conflict — the first cancellation in five years — redirecting to Paris, Mexico City and Manchester instead. Worth flagging directly for a Gulf-based allocator base: geopolitical risk is now visibly repricing where leagues choose to build a Middle East presence.

via Front Office Sports

Worth Watching
  • Women's sports viewership keeps setting records — the WNBA All-Star Game outdrew its NBA counterpart for the first time, and the league reported 73M viewers at midseason versus 63M for all of last season.
  • College sponsorship is repricing above pro comps — Notre Dame ($20M/yr, SoFi) and Ohio State ($17M/yr, JPMorganChase) both signed jersey-patch deals now exceeding the ~$10M average NBA teams get for the same real estate.
  • Active ownership, compounding — VC Mark Ein bought the DC Open in 2019 and has since quadrupled the tournament's revenue with a 100% annual sellout rate.
  • First US women's pro baseball league in 72 years sold out its opener, with ESPN carrying 37 games nationally — an early distribution signal for a brand-new property.
