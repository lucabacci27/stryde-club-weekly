# Stryde Club Weekly
### The business of sport, for allocators — week of Aug 6 – Aug 10, 2026

## Lead: Two majors bought their way into sports-stakes investing this week — only one deal tells you what it actually paid.

On Thursday Brookfield finished absorbing Oaktree, paying $3B for the 26% of the credit giant it didn't already own — a number that cleanly reconciles to an $11.5B valuation for the whole firm, which happens to hold Serie A champion Inter Milan via a 2024 loan default on the club's former Chinese owners. Four days later, on Monday, KKR disclosed that Arctos — the sports-stakes specialist it "recently" acquired — holds $8.8B in minority stakes across teams like the Golden State Warriors, with its two flagship funds generating a reported 22% net return. Set those two numbers side by side and only one is actually a transaction price: Brookfield's $3B bought equity; KKR's $8.8B is the value of the portfolio Arctos already held before the acquisition, not what KKR paid for the manager — a distinction the headline blurs and the story never resolves. No purchase price for Arctos itself, no fee or carry terms, and no denominator for "22% net" (gross of fees? since inception? IRR or MOIC?) made it into the coverage.

The read for allocators: capital is consolidating at the manager level for the winners in sports-stakes investing, not just at the asset level — majors would rather own the platform than assemble a stakes book themselves. But that consolidation is happening only at the top of the market. Underneath it, LIV Golf spent the same three days failing to close a much smaller replacement financing after Saudi PIF's exit: Thursday's story said the league had "secured" an investor and the board had "approved" a term sheet with BC Partners; by Sunday, PIF's own chief was photographed with BC Partners' Ted Goldthorpe at a LIV event, both still described as only "reportedly considering" — the identical hedge, four days on, with no dollar figure ever attached to either version of the story. When capital consolidates cleanly at the manager level for the winners, it gets harder, not easier, to read what a mid-market property is actually being offered.

*(via Sportcal, Google News, Sportico/The Athletic-NYT)*

## Europe

**Arsenal and Emirates re-up at £70M a year, the league's richest shirt deal** — Emirates extended its Arsenal sponsorship through 2033 at £70M annually, a £20M increase on the prior rate, covering stadium, jersey, and training-ground rights in a partnership running since 2006. That's a disclosed, contracted annual figure — unlike this week's Disney Super Bowl estimate below — making it one of the cleaner sponsorship comps available this week for benchmarking European rights growth. *(via SportsPro)*

## Americas

**Portland's arena numbers don't yet agree with each other** — Multnomah County approved $101.6M toward renovating the Moda Center, with the city's own $120M share still pending a vote. Thursday's story pegged the team's total ask at $573M; Monday's follow-up described the same $101.6M as part of a "larger $600M public project," and neither the funding source for the county's share nor the gap between $573M and $600M is reconciled anywhere in the coverage. Owner Tom Dundon is refusing to contribute personally, so the muni-financing gap — not the initial county approval — is the number to watch. *(via NYT/The Athletic, Front Office Sports)*

**DraftKings: handle up, revenue down, and a shot at prediction markets** — Q2 revenue fell 5% to $1.4B even as total wagers jumped 15% to $13.1B, because bettors won more and the company spent heavily promoting its new prediction-markets product; DraftKings held its $900M annual profit target regardless, guidance that's management-asserted, not yet realized. CEO Jason Robins also argued rival prediction-market platforms misrepresent themselves as peer-to-peer when over 90% of their volume allegedly comes from professional trading firms — a claim worth weighing against the fact that DraftKings competes directly with those platforms. *(via Sportico, CompleteIGaming)*

**PENN's online book still isn't paying its way, even after cutting ESPN loose** — PENN posted a record $1.5B in physical casino revenue, funding its online expansion, but that online division lost $9.5M on $349M of sales even after exiting its costly ESPN Bet partnership. Ending an expensive media tie-up was supposed to be the path to online profitability; it isn't there yet, which is the number that actually matters for anyone using PENN as a digital-gaming comp. *(via CompleteIGaming)*

**Super Group's US exit looks vindicated on paper** — Betway parent Super Group swung to a $123M profit from a $3M loss a year ago, with revenue up 18% to $684M and monthly active players up 13% to 6.2M, boosted by World Cup wagering. The read for allocators: exiting a competitive, promo-heavy US market can be the profitable move, not just the defensive one — a useful comp against DraftKings' promo-driven revenue dip above. *(via CompleteIGaming)*

**Nielsen pays $2.15B cash to buy its way into ad verification** — Nielsen is acquiring DoubleVerify, which checks whether digital ads run on legitimate sites, for $2.15B in cash, against DoubleVerify's reported $193.8M in quarterly revenue (roughly 2.8x run-rate revenue if that quarter is representative — unconfirmed). It's a bet that ad-measurement infrastructure, not just content or distribution, is where sports/media rights value increasingly gets captured. *(via Adweek)*

**Callaway's traditional golf business is thriving while LIV fights for survival** — Callaway beat expectations with $612.2M in Q2 revenue, raised its full-year outlook above $2B, grew golf-ball revenue 15% to a record 23% US market share, and is running an active $200M buyback. The contrast with LIV Golf's financing scramble this week is the real story: golf's establishment equipment business is compounding while its disruptive tour model can't close a term sheet. *(via Google News)*

**The Braves' media revenue is the cautionary tale on regional sports networks** — Q2 media revenue fell 10% to $72.85M after the bankruptcy of former broadcaster FanDuel Sports Network forced the team to launch its own network, which the team says is now paying late. As the only publicly traded MLB team, the Braves are the cleanest public read anyone gets on what a regional-network collapse actually costs a franchise in real dollars. *(via Google News)*

**Disney's Super Bowl sellout price is an estimate dressed as a record** — Disney sold out all Super Bowl 2027 ad inventory to 58 brands earlier than any network ever has, with industry estimates (not Disney's own disclosure) pricing a 30-second spot at $8.5-9M and total sales expected to beat NBC's $920M record. Disney itself withheld actual prices, so treat the record-beating claim as directionally likely, not confirmed — still the cleanest signal this week that premium live-sports inventory keeps repricing upward even as regional/cable distribution (see the Braves, above) keeps eroding. *(via Sportico)*

**Under Armour is shrinking its way to hoped-for higher margins** — facing weak US and Asia demand, Under Armour is cutting its product range by 25% to push full-price sales, even as Q2 revenue fell 3% to $1.1B and profit dropped to just $1M. It's a turnaround bet, not a results story yet — the SKU cut is strategy, the $1M profit is this quarter's actual outcome, and allocators should want two more quarters before crediting the strategy. *(via Sportico)*

**WNBA viewership is up 15%, concentrated in one player** — average viewership rose to 756K per game, with Caitlin Clark appearing in all ten of the league's largest audiences since 2000 and one recent game drawing 2.52M viewers. Broad-based audience growth and a single-star-driven spike look identical in an average — the concentration is the caveat before using this as a durable media-rights comp. *(via SportsMediaWatch)*

## Global & Cross-Border

**Brookfield's Oaktree buyout puts Inter Milan back on the market** — Brookfield's $3B purchase of Oaktree's remaining 26% (see lead) also hands it uncontested control of Inter Milan, the Serie A champion Oaktree seized in 2024 after prior owner Suning defaulted on its loan. Reports now suggest Brookfield/Oaktree may sell the club, but no asking price or process has been disclosed — treat any Inter Milan valuation chatter as unsourced until an actual number surfaces. *(via Sportcal)*

**KKR's Arctos numbers are AUM, not a deal price** — see the lead for the basis-check problem with the headline $8.8B figure. KKR/Arctos is already raising a third sports fund and pushing into real estate, so $8.8B is a floor, not a snapshot — worth tracking what multiple KKR is effectively paying once (if ever) an actual purchase price for the Arctos GP surfaces. *(via Google News)*

**LIV Golf's financing is still a term sheet, not a check** — Thursday's story had the league "securing" an unnamed backer and the board "approving" a term sheet, with London PE firm BC Partners weighing a loan to fund the tour through 2030; the restructuring also makes players majority owners, though no equity percentage or valuation was disclosed for what they're receiving. Friday's follow-up revealed a separate move — LIV incorporated Bedminster LLC, a single-event entity that shields assets from creditors amid active US/UK litigation — a liability shield, not new capital, despite running under the same "restructuring" headline. By Sunday, Saudi PIF chief Yasir Al-Rumayyan (the party that pulled funding) was pictured with BC Partners' Ted Goldthorpe at a LIV event, both still merely "considering" — the same non-commitment as Thursday, four days later, with no loan amount, rate, or equity stake ever named. Treat this as an open financing gap, not a completed raise. *(via Sportico, NYT/The Athletic)*

**FIFA's leadership fight keeps splitting along the same regional line** — Infantino held onto executive backing this week despite the fallout from his failed attempt to sell private stakes in World Cup tournaments, but UEFA is maintaining its boycott until FIFA guarantees it won't try again, with the protest now threatening September's U-20 Women's World Cup. A new allegation — that UEFA paid a six-figure sum (amount undisclosed) to a person described as Infantino's former partner, which he denies — has escalated tensions further, while African and Latin American federations continue backing him against European and North American pressure. No deal mechanics here, but the geographic split is itself a data point: World Cup 2027-adjacent commercial rights sit downstream of whoever wins this fight. *(via The Athletic/NYT)*
